- **Positioning:** “fun for everyone,” accessible gameplay, iconic IP, safe/family‑friendly brand

## Slide 4 — Localization & Cultural Adaptation
- Localized hardware bundles, language support, age ratings (CERO/ESRB/PEGI)
- Regional content policies and marketing tone
- Market‑specific partnerships (retail + publishers)
- Example: Japan‑centric IP vs global franchises (Mario/Animal Crossing)

## Slide 5 — Marketing Mix (4Ps) Across Markets
- **Product:** hybrid console + evergreen franchises; region‑specific editions
- **Price:** premium core + value bundles; regional price sensitivity
- **Place:** strong retail presence + eShop digital distribution
- **Promotion:** IP‑led campaigns, influencer/creator marketing, events (Direct, tournaments)

## Slide 6 — International Campaigns & Community Strategy
- Nintendo Direct as global announcement cadence
- Community building: events, demos, esports‑lite competitions
- Leveraging nostalgia while onboarding new audiences
- Cross‑media synergy (movies, merch, theme parks)

## Slide 7 — Challenges, Risks & Lessons
- Supply constraints (hardware shortages) impact global launches
- Currency volatility and regional pricing controversies
- Competition from Sony/Microsoft + mobile gaming
- **Lessons:** strong IP + localized execution = resilient global demand

## Slide 8 — Conclusion (Optional if 7 slides max)
- Nintendo succeeds by combining universal IP with regional adaptation
- Consistent brand promise + controlled ecosystem fuels long‑term loyalty
- International marketing hinges on localization, community, and distribution mix
